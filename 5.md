## Introduction
The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methodology significantly bolsters the cognitive performance of AI models by segmenting complex tasks into more manageable steps. By adopting this prompting strategy, AI models can demonstrate heightened cognitive abilities and offer a deeper understanding of their reasoning processes.

This approach is an example of prompt-based learning, and it requires feeding the model with questions and their corresponding solutions before posing related subsequent questions to it. In other words, our CoT prompt teaches the model to reason about the problem and mimic the same reasoning to respond to further queries correctly.

::page{title="Chain-of-Thought to the rescue"}

Let\'s use the Chain-of-Thought approach to employ the AI\'s reasoning skills.

Our prompt will include a question, its accurate answer giving context and reasoning to the LLM, and then a different question that can be answered using the same line of reasoning.

Let\'s see if it works.

```
Q: An Italian menu has 5 items priced as follows:

- Prosciutto $9.99
- Pecorino $12.99
- Calamari $13.99
- Bruschetta $4.99
- Carpaccio $14.99

Assuming each food is equally filling, spend $30 for a group of people, maximizing satiety.

A: Since each food is equally filling, maximizing our budget will require us to favor items that have a low cost-per-satiety ratio. The ratio of each food, sorted by lowest to highest, is as follows:

- Bruschetta: $4.99
- Prosciutto: $9.99
- Pecorino: $12.99
- Calamari: $13.99
- Carpaccio: $14.99

If we are allowed to have multiple orders of the same item, the simplest answer is doing an integer division of our $30 by the item with the lowest cost per satiety ratio, which is Bruschetta. So we get $30 div $4.99 (the cost of Bruschetta), which is 6. So the answer is an order of 6 Bruschette.

Q: A store sells 4 types of aquarium fish at the following prices:

- Guppies: $3.99
- Goldfish: $1.99
- Betta: $5.99
- Angelfish: $8.99

Spend $20 to maximize the total amount of fish.
```

We can place all this directly in our prompt or split the Chain-of-Thought training in the Prompt Instructions and the actual question in the prompt.

The answer generated should be similar to the following.
> To maximize the number of fish with a fixed budget, buy as many as possible of the cheapest fish.
> 
> **Prices:**
> Guppies: 3.99  
> Goldfish: 1.99  
> Betta: 5.99  
> Angelfish: 8.99  
> 
> **Cheapest item**: Goldfish at 1.99  
> **Max count** = 20 / 1.99 = 10.05 = 10  
> 
> If you buy 10 Goldfish, total cost = 10 × 1.99 = 19.90, leaving 0.10 unspent.  
> 
> Any replacement of a Goldfish by a more expensive type (3.99 or more) would increase the total cost by at least 2.00, making it impossible to keep 10 fish within a 20 budget. Therefore, 10 is the maximum achievable count.
> 
> **Answer**: Buy 10 Goldfish (cost 19.90, leftover 0.10).
> 
> ---
> 
> **Optimal Strategy**
> Always pick the cheapest item when all items count equally toward the objective (more items).
> 
> **Result**
> **Maximum fish**: 10 Goldfish  
> **Total spent**: 19.90  
> **Remaining budget**: 0.10

--- 

Perfect! Exactly what we wanted. The reply offers the correct answer and a proper explanation modeled after our example.

Let\'s consider another example. Use the following prompt (assume the same Prompt Instructions as before about the Italian menu), and check the response of the model.

```
A store sells three box types:

- Small, 10L, $1.99
- Medium, 22L, $4.99
- Large, 38L, $5.99

Spend $20 and maximize volume.
```

The response should be similar to:

> To maximize total volume within a $20 budget when choosing among box types, prefer the box with the best volume-per-dollar ratio and then fill remaining budget with smaller boxes as feasible.
> 
> **Box types:**
> 
> - Small: 10 L, $1.99  
> - Medium: 22 L, $4.99  
> - Large: 38 L, $5.99  
> 
> **Volume per dollar (approx):**
> 
> - Small: 10 / 1.99 ≈ 5.03 L per $  
> - Medium: 22 / 4.99 ≈ 4.41 L per $  
> - Large: 38 / 5.99 ≈ 6.34 L per $  
> 
> Since Large has the best value, maximize the number of Large boxes within $20:
> 
> - Max Large allowed: floor(20 / 5.99) = 3  
> - Cost for 3 Large: 3 × 5.99 = 17.97  
> - Remaining budget: 20.00 − 17.97 = 2.03  
> 
> You can add at most 1 Small (cost 1.99) within the remaining budget.
> 
> **Feasible best with 3 Large:**
> 
> - Small = 1, Medium = 0, Large = 3  
> - Total cost: 1.99 + 3 × 5.99 = 19.96  
> - Total volume: 1 × 10 + 3 × 38 = 124 L  
> - Unspent budget: 20.00 − 19.96 = 0.04 (insufficient for any additional box)  
> 
> **Validation of other possibilities (for completeness):**
> 
> - With 2 Large, the best combinations yield total volumes ≤ 108 L.  
> - With 1 Large, the best combinations yield total volumes ≤ 108 L.  
> - With 0 Large, the best combination (mostly Small) yields ≤ 100 L.  
> 
> **Conclusion: The maximum volume achievable within a $20 budget is 124 L, achieved by:**
> 
> - Small: 1  
> - Medium: 0  
> - Large: 3  
> 
> **Result summary:**
> 
> - Boxes: 1 Small + 3 Large  
> - Total volume: 124 L  
> - Total cost: $19.96 (leaving $0.04 unused)  
> 
> **Optimal mix**
> 
> - Small: 1  
> - Medium: 0  
> - Large: 3  
> - Total volume: 124 L  
> - Total cost: $19.96  

::page{title="Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Prompting"}

Kojima et al. have found a simple, effective approach to improve the results we obtain from our model. This approach is often labeled as a Zero-Shot CoT.

There are a few words that, when added to the prompt, are likely to solicit better answers since they invite the AI to do step-by-step reasoning, much such as a human would when trying to come to a resolution.

According to researchers, two effective phrases are:

> Let\'s think step by step.

And:

> Let\'s work this out in a step-by-step way to be sure we have the right answer.

These words are helpful but not magic. So feel free to use them to improve your results, but they are usually best used along with other techniques discuss in this course.

For example, tucking these words at the end of our standard prompt generates an incorrect answer with the GPT 3.5 model.

**Prompt**

```
Let's think step by step.

An Italian menu has 5 items, each equally satisfying, priced as follows:

- Prosciutto $9.99
- Pecorino $12.99
- Calamari $13.99
- Bruschetta $4.99
- Carpaccio $14.99

Considering a budget of $30, what should the money be spent on to maximize satiety ?
```

**Output**
>To maximize satiety within a $30 budget, we need to select a combination of menu items whose total cost does not exceed $30 and yields the highest total satisfaction.
>
>Assuming each item provides equal satisfaction, the goal simplifies to maximizing the total number of items purchased without exceeding the budget.
>
>Let\'s analyze the options:
>
>| Item | Price |
| -- | -- |
| Prosciutto | 9.99 |
| Pecorino | 12.99 |
| Calamari | 13.99 |
| Bruschetta | 4.99 |
| Carpaccio | 14.99 |
>
>Since Bruschetta is the cheapest, buying multiple Bruschetta items could maximize the number of items, but we need to consider if combining different items yields better satisfaction.
>
>Given that all items are equally satisfying, the optimal strategy is to buy as many inexpensive items as possible.
>
>Let\'s calculate the maximum number of Bruschetta we can buy:
>
>Budget: $30
>Price of Bruschetta: $4.99
?Max quantity: 30 / 4.99 ≈ 6 items, total cost ≈ 29.94
?Remaining budget: 30 - 29.94 ≈ 0.06, insufficient to buy another item.
>
>Total satisfaction: 6 items.
>
>Alternatively, buying a mix of items might yield more satisfaction if items are equally satisfying. But since all items are equally satisfying, and we want to maximize the number of items, buying only Bruschetta maximizes quantity.
>
>Therefore, the optimal choice is to buy 6 Bruschetta items, spending approximately $29.94.

In other words, our Chain-of-Thought approach illustrated before, in which we use our prompt to \"teach\" the model the desired outcome, is better.

Still, Zero-Shot CoT is useful whenever you want quick, elaborate, step-by-step reasoning without needing to supply full reasoning examples. It&#96;s especially handy for generating longer responses, such as blog posts, essays, or guides.

::page{title="Chain-of-Thought to explore subjects"}

Chain-of-Thought can be used in various ways to improve the chatbot\'s reasoning, especially in areas where it\'s feeble. However, a more valuable use is when it comes to exploring subjects more in-depth.

Instead of asking a generic question, we can break it down into steps we want the model to consider to develop a much richer and valuable answer.

Let\'s see a practical example that doesn\'t necessarily use the question-and-answer template format.

For example, let\'s ask the model to tell us about Space Exploration:

```
What is space exploration?
```

Now, let\'s try a Chain-of-Thought approach. Start a new chat and place the following in the Prompt Instructions:

```
Cover each and every one of the following elements in your answer.

- Historic Space Missions
- Moon landing and Human Achievement
- Moon landing and impact on the Cold War
- Satellite technology and its impact on humanity
- Mars colonization possibilities
- Search for extraterrestrial life
- Space tourism prospects
- Space debris and environmental impact
- International Space Station collaboration
- Advancements in rocket technology
- Interstellar travel challenges
- Private companies and Billionaires involvement controversy

Give a comprehensive response that covers all points in depth.
```

We are giving the model a lot of items to consider in its answer and tucking in Kojima et al.\'s suggestion of inviting the model to take a step-by-step approach for good measure.

What a huge difference!

The downside is that we had to develop a list requiring knowledge of the subject or at least research into it, and this is time-consuming.

On the plus side, we didn\'t have to retrain the model, which would be truly time-consuming and potentially expensive. Instead, the prompt split the \"problem\" into smaller steps worth exploring and leveraged the existing model training to compute a reply.

Moreover, these starting points can lead to various interconnected thoughts and ideas from the model. The beauty of a Chain-of-Thought is that it can branch out in different directions, exploring numerous aspects and perspectives related to the initial topic.

We can ask specific questions at any time after the model has already shown us a broader understanding of the topic.
