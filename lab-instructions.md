## Introduction

In this course, you learned that the relevance, coherence, and accuracy of responses generated by generative AI models largely depend on the prompts. You also explored various techniques, approaches, and best practices in prompt engineering to craft effective prompts that leverage a model’s capabilities to the fullest and generate the desired responses.

In this project, you will recap your learning on prompt engineering approaches and best practices for writing impactful prompts.

## Learning Objectives
1. Apply prompt engineering approaches to create effective prompts based on specific requirements.  

2. Apply best practices for writing effective prompts across the dimensions of clarity, context, and precision.

Consider the following prompt designed by a student pursuing graduation in computer science. The student wants to explore the career options in the field of computer science. 

```
Please guide me on potential career paths in the field of computer science, considering my interests, skills, the evolving technology landscape, and the impact of AI, while also factoring in work-life balance and opportunities for personal growth.
```

This prompt is ambiguous and complex because it includes a wide range of career-related aspects without specifying the student\'s specific interests, skills, or personal goals. It also combines considerations related to technology trends, AI\'s impact, work-life balance, and personal growth opportunities, making it challenging to provide precise career guidance.

### Task
Consider yourself in the role of this student. Your task is to design clear and precise prompt(s), considering required assumptions regarding your interests, skills, or personal goals. For example, you may assume that you have a strong interest in machine learning and natural language processing and have good programming skills.

2. Write the following instructions in the **Prompt Instructions** field. 
```
I am pursuing a degree in Computer Science and exploring career opportunities for myself after graduation.  
```
3. Design the prompt(s), considering the best practices for clarity and precision.

>- Quantitative Online Surveys (Core Demographics): Large-scale surveys to quantify who the premium handset is likely to attract across regions. Use representative panels to ensure insights map to target markets.
>- Qualitative In-Depth Interviews (IDIs): One-on-one conversations with carefully screened respondents to uncover motivations, values, and barriers that demographic data alone can't reveal. Useful for refining personas (Creator, Executive, Ultra-premium Tech Enthusiast).
>- Ethnographic/Diary Studies (Deep Context): Short-term diary/journal studies or in-store ethnography to capture real-world usage, buying triggers, and the situational context in which premium devices are considered.
>- Intercept & In-Store Surveys (Offline): Short surveys at premium retail locations, launches, or branded experiences to capture shopper demographics, intent, and immediate perceptions.
>- Quick Pulse/Event Surveys (Hybrid): Rapid feedback during live events, showroom tours, or VIP pre-orders to gauge sentiment, excitement, and channel preferences in near real time.
>- Conjoint/Discrete Choice Experiments (DCE): Quantify how respondents trade off features, pricing, and ecosystem benefits to prioritize product attributes for premium segments.
>- Message & Concept Testing (A/B Testing): Pre-test positioning statements, taglines, and visuals with both online and offline audiences to ensure consistent resonance across channels.
>- Privacy-First Data Collection & Compliance: Design all surveys with GDPR/CCPA awareness, clear consent, and data minimization. Provide opt-out options and anonymized reporting.

>**2) Key Demographic Parameters to Capture**

>- Age bands: 18-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65+ (with regional adjustments). Focus on segments most likely to buy a premium flagship: 25-54 as core, with insights on 18-24 (early adopters) and 55-64 (premium lifestyle buyers).
>- Income & Wealth Indicators: Household annual income brackets tailored by region (e.g., USD equivalents). Include current asset ownership (home, car, premium devices) as a proxy for purchasing power.
>- Occupation & Social Role: Professionals, executives, designers/creatives, tech engineers, entrepreneurs, students. Capture decision-making power (self-purchase vs. gifted), and status signaling.
>- Education: High school, some college, bachelor’s, master’s or higher. Indicates value placed on design/tech sophistication.
>- Geography & Urban Form: Country/region, city tier (Tier 1, Tier 2, suburban, rural). Urban density and travel patterns influence store strategy and event design.
>- Device Ownership & Tech Readiness: Current smartphone model, age of device, ownership tenure, tech-curiosity level (early adopter vs. pragmatic user), willingness to upgrade for prestige vs. functionality.
>- Brand Affinity & Luxury Orientation: Affinity for premium or luxury brands, past purchases (luxury watches, fashion, premium audio), and perceived status signaling from devices.
>- Channel Preference & Purchase Path: Online vs. offline shopping preference, willingness to pre-order, trust in carrier vs. direct-to-consumer channels, and past experiences with premium launches.
>- Usage Context & Lifestyle: Professional usage (heavy camera work, video calls, productivity), travel frequency, fitness/apparel alignment, sustainability values, and repair/lasting-value priorities.
>- Sustainability & Ethical Values (psychographic): Importance of ethical sourcing, durability, repairability, and lifecycle impact in purchase decisions.
>- Language & Cultural Nuances: Primary language, cultural considerations, and messaging sensitivities that affect tone and visuals.
>- Customer Loyalty & Lifetime Value Attitudes: Attitude toward warranties, trade-ins, software updates, and long-term ecosystem investments.

>**3) Survey Execution Plan (Online + Offline)**

>- Timeline: 6–10 weeks total (4 weeks for qualitative and 4–6 weeks for quantitative), with iterative findings feeding Step 1 messaging and personas.
>- Sample Size Targets:
>- Online surveys: 2,000–3,000 completed per major region (US/EU/APAC) to enable segmentation; 1,000–1,500 for smaller markets.
>- IDIs: 40–60 interviews per region, ensuring representation across ages, incomes, and professions.
>- In-store intercepts: 300–600 short surveys per region across flagship stores and pop-ups.
>- Diary/ethnography: 20–30 households per region for 2–4 weeks.
>- Recruitment & Panels: Use multiple sources (premium panel providers, existing CRM with consented profiling, showroom sign-ups, targeted social ads) to ensure a diverse but premium-leaning sample. Screen for design affinity, premium purchase history, and sustainability values to align with the three anchor personas.
>- Questionnaire Design Principles:
>- Start with screening questions to ensure respondents are in target segments.
>- Use balanced scales (e.g., 5–7 point Likert) for attitudes toward design, performance, and sustainability.
>- Include open-text prompts for uncovering latent needs and emotions around premium devices.
>- Incorporate attitudinal questions that map to the three personas (Creator, Executive, Ultra-premium Tech Enthusiast).
>- Include behavioral questions about purchase triggers, preferred channels, and willingness to pay for ecosystem advantages.
>- Add validation questions to check consistency and reduce straight-lining.
>- Data Handling & Privacy:
>- Obtain explicit consent for each survey, with a privacy notice detailing data use.
>- Anonymize responses and store personally identifiable information separately, per regional laws.
>- Provide opt-out and data deletion options; report aggregate findings only.
>- Analysis & Outputs:
>- Segmentation: cluster respondents into persona-aligned segments with demographic and psychographic profiles.
>- Persona dossiers: Creator, Executive, Ultra-premium Tech Enthusiast, including demographics, motivations, messaging preferences, and channel habits.
>- Key metrics: share of premium-seeking respondents, price sensitivity, willingness to pre-order, and favorite channels for learning about new devices.
>- Decision-ready artifacts: messaging maps, audience targets for online and offline activations, and recommended product feature prioritization based on audience value.
