## Introduction
The tree of thoughts (ToT) approach is a prompting strategy designed to enhance the reasoning abilities of large language models by structuring thinking as a tree-like process. Instead of generating a single linear response, the model is encouraged to explore multiple reasoning paths, evaluate them, and select the most promising one. This allows for more deliberate and interpretable decision-making, especially in complex or open-ended tasks.

By breaking a problem into intermediate steps and branching out into alternative ideas or strategies at each stage, ToT enables deeper exploration. It supports critical thinking, planning, and reflection by allowing paths to be compared and refined. The approach mirrors how humans often reason, by weighing different possibilities before converging on a conclusion—making it highly effective for creative, analytical, or ambiguous problems.

## Example 1: Planning a fundraising event

Consider a scenario where you have been made in charge of a fundraiser being organized by your school and you are using a GenAI system to plan it out. You can query your GenAI system using the prompt below.

```
You are planning a school fundraising event.
Follow this structure:

List three different types of events (label them A, B, C).

For each event, list:
    a. Key benefits
    b. Likely challenges
    c. What resources would be needed

Compare the three events and choose the most feasible one. Explain why it is better than the others.
```

Here, the tree of thoughts (ToT) approach is used to systematically explore multiple event ideas—each developed into a thought branch with sub-criteria like benefits, challenges, and required resources. This example shows how ToT encourages **structured divergence** before making a decision. Rather than settling on the first plausible idea, the model is prompted to **generate and evaluate multiple possibilities**.

This structured branching not only leads to a more informed and creative outcome (e.g., selecting a student-led talent show over conventional options) but also provides **transparency in reasoning**. It demonstrates the strength of ToT in helping models (and humans) avoid tunnel vision, compare trade-offs, and make decisions grounded in thoughtful analysis. For learners, it highlights how ToT supports **deliberate thinking over default thinking**, which is crucial in open-ended or planning-heavy tasks.

## Example 2: Designing a family meal plan on a budget
You have decided to take a renewed approach toward improving the health of your family, but are feeling confused between different approaches of eating healthy. You can use the model to design a weekly meal plan for your family based on one of three different approaches that appeal to you. Consider the following prompt.

```
You are helping a family of four plan healthy meals for the week on a tight budget.
Follow this structure:

Propose three different meal plan strategies (label them A, B, C). For example:
    - A: Vegetarian-heavy meals
    - B: Mix of bulk grains and frozen foods
    - C: One-pot meals to reduce prep

For each strategy, list:
    a. Estimated cost per day
    b. Nutritional strengths
    c. Convenience level (prep time, cleanup)

Compare the three strategies and choose the best one. Explain why it's the most practical based on cost, nutrition, and convenience.
```

This example highlights how the tree of thoughts (ToT) approach supports practical decision-making when balancing multiple priorities—in this case, cost, nutrition, and convenience. Instead of generating a single solution, the model is guided to explore three distinct planning strategies, such as vegetarian meals, bulk ingredient use, and one-pot cooking. Each strategy is then broken down into specific factors: daily cost, nutritional value, and ease of preparation.

This example shows the power of ToT in everyday planning by encouraging structured exploration, not just quick answers. By comparing each option against real-world constraints, the model can reason more deliberately and arrive at a well-justified recommendation. For learners, it reinforces how ToT helps in thinking beyond the obvious, evaluating trade-offs, and making choices that fit real needs—even in something as routine as planning meals.

The expected output will be as follows.

>Here are three distinct meal plan strategies tailored for a family of four on a tight budget, each with its own advantages:
> ## Budget-Friendly Meal Plan Strategies for a Family of Four
>
> ### A. Vegetarian-Heavy Meals
> **Estimated cost per day:** $12–16  
>
> **Nutritional strengths:**  
> - High fiber from beans, lentils, whole grains, and vegetables  
> - Good plant-based protein from legumes; can include dairy or eggs if desired  
> - Rich in vitamins and minerals from a variety of vegetables  
> - Typically lower in saturated fat and cholesterol  
>
> **Convenience level (prep time and cleanup):**  
> - Medium: requires planning to combine protein sources with grains  
> - Batch-cooking components helps  
> - More cooking steps than one-pot meals but still manageable  
>
> ---
>
> ### B. Mix of Bulk Grains and Frozen Foods
> **Estimated cost per day:** $10–14  
>
> **Nutritional strengths:**  
> - Balanced macros with steady sources of carbohydrates, protein, and fats  
> - Frozen vegetables provide convenient, nutritious additions with good shelf life  
> - Flexible protein options (eggs, beans, dairy, canned fish) keep costs predictable  
>
> **Convenience level (prep time and cleanup):**  
> - Medium-High: easier prep using bulk grains and pre-cut or frozen items  
> - Batch cook grains and mix with proteins/vegetables for quick meals  
>
> ---
>
> ### C. One-Pot Meals to Reduce Prep
> **Estimated cost per day:** $9–13  
>
> **Nutritional strengths:**  
> - Combines legumes/whole grains with vegetables for fiber and micronutrients  
> - Easily designed to be meat-lean or vegetarian while providing complete meals  
> - Reduces waste by using pantry staples; supports steady energy through the day  
>
> **Convenience level (prep time and cleanup):**  
> - High: minimal cookware and cleanup  
> - Simple cooking steps; great for busy weeknights  
>
> ---
>
> ### Comparison and Recommendation
>
> **Quick cost snapshot:**  
> - C (One-pot): lowest daily cost ($9–13)  
> - B (Bulk grains + frozen): moderate ($10–14)  
> - A (Vegetarian-heavy): highest ($12–16)  
>
> **Nutritional flexibility:**  
> - A: Strong fiber and plant-protein focus; needs planning to cover all essential amino acids if limiting dairy/eggs  
> - B: Reliable protein and vegetables with less meal-by-meal planning  
> - C: Can include full nutrients by rotating legumes, grains, and vegetables  
>
> **Convenience and planning effort:**  
> - C: Most convenient (minimal dishes, fast cleanup)  
> - B: Moderately convenient (batch-cooked grains + frozen items)  
> - A: Requires more planning for protein adequacy and variety  
>
> **Recommendation:**  
> - **Primary pick: C – One-Pot Meals**  
>   - Best balance of low cost, good nutrition, and maximum convenience.  
>   - Simplifies weeknight cooking, reduces cleanup, and supports variety by rotating spices, vegetables, and legumes.  
>
> - **Alternative blends:**  
>   - Add B (bulk grains + frozen veg) some days for even lower daily prep.  
>   - Keep C (one-pot meals) as the core most days of the week.  
>
> ---
>
> ### Implementation Tips
> - Start with 2–3 core one-pot recipes (e.g., lentil chili, chickpea curry, veggie sausage & white bean with greens).  
> - Build a pantry list: lentils/beans, brown rice or quinoa, canned tomatoes, frozen vegetables, onions, garlic, spices, affordable proteins (eggs, canned fish, tofu).  
> - Batch-cook on weekends or evenings: prepare a large pot of grains and legumes; portion and freeze extras.  
> - Rotate ingredients (greens, peppers, carrots, root vegetables) to keep meals interesting and nutritionally varied.  
> - Use sales and seasonal produce to lower costs; substitute with cheaper staples when available.  
> - Track weekly food costs to tailor meals to local prices and reduce waste.

1. **Crafting a plot twist for a short story**

	**Domain:** Creative writing

	**Scenario:**
	You\'re writing a mystery short story and want to include a compelling twist at the end that surprises the reader but still makes logical sense.
  **Context:**  
The story is about a detective investigating a missing person in a small town. The tone is mostly realistic, but the twist can involve deception, misdirection, or hidden motives.

  **Learner Task:**  
Design a prompt that encourages the model to follow a structured reasoning approach:

1. Instruct the model to generate three distinct plot twist ideas relevant to the story and label them clearly.  
2. Guide the model to examine each twist in detail by considering factors such as its impact on the reader, how well it aligns with earlier story elements, its thematic contribution, its level of originality, and how effectively it can be set up through foreshadowing.  
3. Direct the model to compare the three options based on their strengths and overall fit with the story, and then select the most effective twist with a clear justification of why it enhances the story’s impact. 

<details><Summary>Click here for a sample prompt</Summary>
You are helping an author come up with a compelling twist ending for a short mystery story.

The story is about a detective investigating a missing person in a small town. The story is mostly realistic, but the twist can involve deception, misdirection, or hidden motives.

Follow this structure:

1. Generate 3 different plot twist ideas. Label them A, B, and C.
(Example types: the detective is being misled, the victim staged their own disappearance, or a seemingly irrelevant character is central to the mystery.)

2. For each twist, explain the following:
	
	a. Why it would surprise the reader

	b. How it connects to earlier parts of the story

	c. What emotional or thematic impact it creates

3. Evaluate the three twists:
    
	- Which is most original?
    
	- Which fits best with the tone of the story?
    
	- Which would leave the strongest impression on the reader?

4. Choose the best twist and justify your choice.

2. **Fixing a decline in customer satisfaction**

	**Domain:** Business/customer support

	**Scenario:**
	A company notices that its customer satisfaction score has dropped significantly in the past quarter. They want to understand why and how to fix it.

	**Learner task:**
	Write a ToT-style prompt that guides the model to:
	 - Suggest three likely causes (e.g., delayed support, product bugs, and unclear policies)
	 - Outline supporting data to look for, the impact on users, and a potential fix for each cause
	 - Recommend the root cause and the highest-priority fix

<details><summary>Click here for a sample prompt</summary>
Your company has noticed that customer satisfaction scores have dropped steadily over the last three months. You don't know the exact cause yet, but you have access to support tickets, customer reviews, and internal team feedback.

Follow this structure:

1. Identify 3 possible causes of the decline. Label them A, B, and C.
(Examples might include: longer response times, product bugs, or changes in pricing.)

2. For each possible cause, explain the following:
	
	a. What evidence might support this cause?
	
	b. How would this issue affect customer satisfaction?
	
	c. What steps could be taken to investigate or fix it?

3. Compare the three causes:
	
	- Which is most likely based on the data?
	
	- Which would have the biggest impact if resolved?

4. Recommend a focused next step:
	Choose the most likely root cause and describe an actionable plan to address it.
	
</details>

3. **Planning a gap year abroad**
	
	**Domain:** Personal planning/travel

	**Scenario:**
	A student is taking a gap year and wants to make the most of it through travel, volunteering, or learning new skills, but can only choose one main focus.

	**Learner task:**
	Design a prompt that asks the model to:
	
	 - Outline three different approaches (e.g., travel across Asia, join a conservation program, take a coding bootcamp)
	 - For each, list potential costs, personal growth opportunities, and long-term benefits
	
	- Select the most meaningful plan based on the student\'s goals

<details><summary>Click here for a sample prompt</summary>
A student is taking a gap year before starting college or work. They want to make the most of this time through travel, volunteering, or learning new skills—but can only choose one main focus.

Help them decide by following this structure:

1. Propose 3 possible plans. Label them A, B, and C.
	(Examples:
	
	A. Travel across Southeast Asia
	
	B. Volunteer for a wildlife conservation program
	
	C. Take an intensive coding bootcamp)

2. For each plan, describe:
	
	a. What the student will do
	
	b. What they will gain from it (personal, professional, or educational)
	
	c. Challenges or trade-offs (cost, effort, missed opportunities)

3. Compare the three plans:
	
	- Which plan offers the most long-term value?
	
	- Which aligns best with the student\'s goals or interests?
	
	- Which feels most personally fulfilling or exciting?

4. Recommend one plan and justify the choice clearly.
